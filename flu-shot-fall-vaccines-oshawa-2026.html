Flu Shot, COVID-19 and RSV: Your Fall 2026 Vaccine Guide for Oshawa Families

Ontario's free fall vaccine program opens in stages every autumn. Here is who should get what, when, and where in Oshawa, including how to bring the whole family in one visit.

By the team at Simcoe Medical Clinic September 16, 20268 minute read

Dates to confirm. Ontario publishes the exact start dates for its fall vaccine program each September. The timing below reflects how the province has run the program in recent years. We will update this page as soon as the 2026 dates are confirmed, and you can always call us at (905) 215-1815 to ask whether your vaccine is available yet.

The short version

  • Ontario's free fall vaccine program usually opens to high risk groups in late September and to everyone else in late October.
  • Everyone six months and older should get a flu shot every year. It is free in Ontario, and an enhanced version is available for adults 65 and older.
  • The updated COVID-19 vaccine is recommended each fall, and you can get it in the same visit as your flu shot.
  • RSV immunization is publicly funded for babies entering their first RSV season, for adults 75 and older, and for some adults 60 and older at higher risk.
  • Children under two need their flu shot from a doctor or public health clinic, not a pharmacy. We see them.
  • Fall is also the time to catch children up on the routine vaccines Ontario schools require.

Respiratory virus season in Ontario runs roughly from October to April, and it arrives on schedule whether or not we are ready for it. Three vaccines do most of the work of keeping families out of the emergency department over the winter: the flu shot, the updated COVID-19 vaccine, and, for the youngest and oldest among us, RSV.

This guide covers who should get each one, when the province makes them available, and where to get them in Oshawa. If you only read one line, read this: book one visit for the whole family in late October, and bring your health cards.

When fall vaccines start in Ontario

Ontario rolls its fall program out in two waves. In the first wave, usually the last week of September, the updated COVID-19 vaccine and the flu shot become available to people at higher risk of severe illness: adults 65 and older, residents of long term care and retirement homes, pregnant people, children under five, people with chronic conditions or weakened immune systems, Indigenous people and their household members, and health care workers. In the second wave, usually the last week of October, both vaccines open to everyone six months and older.

The timing is deliberate. Protection takes about two weeks to build and is strongest in the first few months, and flu in Ontario typically peaks between December and February. A shot in late October or November covers the peak. If you miss that window, get vaccinated anyway. The season runs into spring.

The flu shot

Influenza is not a bad cold. Every winter it sends thousands of Ontarians to hospital, most of them very young, over 65, pregnant, or living with a condition like asthma, diabetes, or heart disease. The vaccine is updated every year to match the strains expected to circulate, which is why last year's shot does not count.

Who should get it. Everyone six months and older, every year. It matters most for the high risk groups above, and for anyone who lives with or cares for them, including parents of babies too young to be vaccinated.

A few details worth knowing. Adults 65 and older can ask for the enhanced flu vaccine that Ontario funds for seniors, which produces a stronger immune response. Children between six months and eight years who have never had a flu shot before need two doses about four weeks apart, so it pays to start early. And an egg allergy is no longer a reason to skip the flu shot; it can be given safely.

The updated COVID-19 vaccine

COVID-19 has settled into a pattern that looks a lot like flu: it circulates all year and surges in the fall and winter. Ontario now offers an updated vaccine each autumn, matched to the variants currently spreading, and recommends it for everyone six months and older, with the strongest recommendation for the same high risk groups as flu. Only people in those high risk groups are offered an additional spring dose, so the fall dose is the one most families should plan around.

You can get the COVID-19 vaccine and the flu shot on the same day, one in each arm. It is safe, it is what public health recommends, and it saves you a second trip.

RSV for infants and older adults

Respiratory syncytial virus, or RSV, is a cold for most people and a serious illness for two groups: babies in their first year, for whom it is the leading cause of hospitalization in Canada, and adults over 75 or with certain health conditions. Ontario funds RSV protection for both.

  • Babies. Infants entering their first RSV season can receive a single injection of a long acting antibody that protects them through the winter. It is given by a doctor, a hospital before discharge, or a public health clinic. Alternatively, a vaccine given to the mother between 32 and 36 weeks of pregnancy passes protection to the baby.
  • Older adults. Adults 75 and older qualify for a publicly funded RSV vaccine, as do adults 60 to 74 who live in long term care, retirement homes or chronic care, or who have certain conditions such as organ transplant or dialysis. Unlike the flu shot, the RSV vaccine is not repeated every year. One dose provides protection for at least two seasons.

If you are not sure whether you or your parent qualifies, ask at your visit. Eligibility has expanded in recent years and may change again this fall.

Children and back to school

September puts vaccines on every parent's mind for a second reason. Ontario schools require a record on file with the local public health unit showing that each student is vaccinated against diphtheria, tetanus, polio, measles, mumps, rubella, meningococcal disease and whooping cough, plus chickenpox for children born in 2010 or later. Durham Region Health Department reviews these records and sends notices to families with gaps, which can lead to suspension if not resolved.

If your child is missing a dose, or if you arrived from another country and are not sure how the records line up, book a visit. Our physicians can compare your child's record to the Ontario schedule, give any catch up doses, and add the flu shot at the same time. Newcomer families can also read our newcomer's guide to health care in Ontario, which explains how to report records to public health.

Your fall check-in if you live with a chronic condition

If you have diabetes, asthma or COPD, heart or kidney disease, or a weakened immune system, or if you are 65 or older, fall vaccines are part of a larger routine. Use the same visit to review your medications, check your blood pressure and blood sugar, and make sure any screening that is due gets booked. A single flu infection can undo months of good control in these conditions, and a thirty minute appointment in October is far easier than a week in hospital in January. Our doctors provide ongoing chronic disease care and are happy to combine it with your vaccines.

What to expect afterwards

Most people have a sore arm for a day or two. Some feel tired, achy or have a low fever for a day, particularly after the COVID-19 vaccine. These are signs of the immune system doing its job, and they pass. A cool cloth on the arm and moving the arm normally both help. You cannot catch the flu from the flu shot; the vaccine does not contain live virus.

Serious reactions are very rare, which is why you are asked to stay for fifteen minutes after your shot. Call us if a reaction seems severe or lasts more than a few days, and call 911 for any trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or a fast spreading rash.

Booking your family's vaccines at Simcoe Medical Clinic

You can walk in during clinic hours or book online, choosing a male or female physician. If you are bringing several family members, booking lets us set aside enough time and avoid separate waits. Bring everyone's health card, wear short sleeves, and let us know if a child is getting their first ever flu shot so we can schedule the second dose. We are open Monday to Friday from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM and Saturday from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM, and Arabic is spoken at the clinic.
